# Raptor Emergency Management
I worked for a company called Raptor Technologies that is in the emergency management space.  I was brought in to work on the CEO's vision.  He had envisioned a system that would allow you to automate the [Emergency Response Plan](https://www.ready.gov/business/implementation/emergency) (ERP) and keep our schools safe.  I can say now, that man truely had a vision, and was working in a space that was needed.

The system was "simple".  Start an Incident based on a type from the ERP, account for the children.  As all developers know what is simple is never as simple as it sounds.  The important part is we made it work.  There were good and bad parts about that job, like any other.  The pressure of being first to market with new software.  The reality this is the CEO's vision and we needed to pull it off. All while adding over 2000 unit tests to, an otherwise, untested system.  Looking back, it was some of the best software I've written to date for a variety of reasons.  The main one being the genesis of this article.

### Software as a Service
As a developer, I struggle with aspects of our industry.  Fixed Deadlines without an understanding of the effort to accomplish the task.  Developers being blamed when there is supposed to be the same tri-balanced system of checks an balances that makes America so great.  The Holy Trinity of Software. The Order Specification, The Order Implementation, and The Order Verification.

Stop me when it hurts?  Sales sells a feature that we don't have.  The delivery team is tasked with the impossible with no movement on deadline or quality.  So we dig deep and find a way to make it the best we can, because we like having a job.  

This was not that experience.  We were issued a task, and it wasn't unreasonable.  Deliver an application that satisfies the mission.  For once the time sensitivity wasn't a facticious date that somebody decided to satisfy montary concerns.  The time to get this thing out might be the difference between save lives or not.  

### The Mission
While struggles existed on this software, the trinity was aligned towards the vision the CEO laid out.  Keep Children Safe.  I am not trying to paint a positive or negative view of this software delivery process.  Its software, what I am pointing out is the vision was clear.  Deliver a product that was maintainable, scalable and would satisfy a very real need, Keep Children Safe.

From this perspective you will hear me talk about managers and leaders.  There were nothing but passionate leaders on this team, aligned towards a goal, Keep Children Safe.  We worked, struggled, and grew together to accomplish the task we set out to do.  Keep Children Safe.

# The Fullfilment

A few months after the Uvalde school shooting, I got a call from my former Product Manager.  We spoke, a brief moment catching up on how the company was doing, how he was doing, how I was doing.  Normal stuff people do.  Then he tells me the purpose of the call.  He wanted me to know, given my involvement and commitment to that project, some information.  Raptor EM was used during the incident.  My heart sunk into my stomach.  My mind racing.  "Were there bugs?", "Did the software work?", "Was it responsible for helping or harming in the situation?".  Prayers answered.  It was used, it worked, and it possibly saved lifes.

I have never felt more pride in my profession than in that moment.
